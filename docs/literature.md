## Al Massira and Moroccan reservoirs

**Monitoring water crisis from space across a Mediterranean region**
Wassim Mohamed Baba, Abdelghani Chehbouni, Youness Ouassanouan, Simon Gascoin,
Marc Paganini, Giuseppe Ottavianelli, Zoltan Szantoi — *Scientific Reports*, 2025
— [10.1038/s41598-025-06240-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-06240-1)
Tracks surface water area of Moroccan reservoirs with Sentinel-1 radar, 2018–2024,
finding Al Massira and Mansour Eddahbi declining sharply from mid-2021 and
attributing it to drought plus irrigation abstraction. **This is the closest
published work to our MVP** — it is the baseline to beat or complement.

**Use of Spatial Remote Sensing to Study the Temporal Evolution of the Water
Retention of Al Massira Dam in Morocco**
Mohamed Bounif, Abdelkrim Rahimi, Rachid Boutafoust, Ikram El Mjiri —
*Journal of Ecological Engineering*, 2023 —
[10.12911/22998993/156666](https://doi.org/10.12911/22998993/156666)
Estimates Al Massira's lake surface from Landsat imagery and spectral indices and
relates it to measured water retention. Only the abstract was readable.

**Combined use of Sentinel-2 and Landsat-8 to monitor water surface area and
evaluated drought risk severity using Google Earth Engine**
Brahim Benzougagh, Sarita Meshram, Bouchta El Fellah, Mohamed Mastere, Abdallah
Dridri, Driss Sadkaoui, Khalid Mimich, Khaled Khedher — *Earth Science
Informatics*, 2022 —
[10.1007/s12145-021-00761-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12145-021-00761-9)
Combines Sentinel-2 and Landsat-8 in Google Earth Engine to track reservoir
surface area and score drought risk. Methodologically the nearest analogue to our
optical approach; only the abstract was readable, so confirm the study area
before citing it as Moroccan.

## Morocco drought: meteorological, hydrological and vegetation indices

**Integrated drought assessment in Morocco: a combined approach using
meteorological, hydrological, and remote sensing observations (1975–2023)**
Fatima Ouelkabir, Atman Ait Lamqadem, Laila Saafadi, Biswajeet Pradhan, Guoqiang
Wang, Babak Ghazi, Karim Charef, Younes Laabdi — *Modeling Earth Systems and
Environment*, 2026 — [10.1007/s40808-026-02815-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40808-026-02815-7)
Combines SPI, a streamflow drought index and Landsat NDVI over the Ahmed El
Hansali dam basin (upper Oum Er-Rbia) for ~50 years, and claims to be the first
to join all three for that basin.

**Assessing the Impact of Agricultural Practices and Urban Expansion on Drought
Dynamics Using a Multi-Drought Index Application Implemented in Google Earth
Engine: A Case Study of the Oum Er-Rbia Watershed, Morocco**
Imane Serbouti, Jérôme Chenal, Biswajeet Pradhan, El Bachir Diop, Rida Azmi,
Seyid Abdellahi Ebnou Abdem, Meriem Adraoui, Mohammed Hlal, Mariam Bounabi —
*Remote Sensing*, 2024 — [10.3390/rs16183398](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16183398)
Multi-index drought assessment of the Oum Er-Rbia watershed — Al Massira's own
basin — for 2002–2022, attributing drought dynamics partly to irrigation and
urban expansion.

**Assessment of drought variability in the Marrakech-Safi region (Morocco) at
different time scales using GIS and remote sensing**
Chaimae Elair, Khalid Rkha Chaham, Abdelghani Hadri — *Water Supply*, 2023 —
[10.2166/ws.2023.283](https://doi.org/10.2166/ws.2023.283)
Multi-timescale drought analysis for Marrakech-Safi; its VCI results make 2016 a
notably severe agricultural-drought year (reported as 57% of area in extreme
drought).

**Monitoring of agricultural drought using remote sensing data in the Sebou
watershed, Morocco**
Abdelhak El-Bouhali, Khadija El Ouazani Ech-Chahdi, Mhamed Amyay — *E3S Web of
Conferences*, 2025 — [10.1051/e3sconf/202560704006](https://doi.org/10.1051/e3sconf/202560704006)
MODIS NDVI/VCI-based agricultural drought monitoring in the Sebou basin, reporting
~50% of the basin in extreme drought in 2016.

**Monitoring and Assessment Drought Dynamics in High and Middle Moulouya,
Morocco, Using Remote Sensing and Meteorological-Based Indices**
Ali Salem, Mustapha Amiri, Yasir Abduljaleel, Ehab Amen, Zana Ali, Ahmed Awad,
Mohamed Ghzal — *Advances in Science, Technology & Innovation* (Springer), 2024 —
[10.1007/978-3-031-48715-6_25](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-48715-6_25)
Drought dynamics in the Moulouya basin from combined remote sensing and
meteorological indices. Abstract only.

## Groundwater (GRACE and InSAR)

**When climate variability partly compensates for groundwater depletion: An
analysis of the GRACE signal in Morocco**
Hamza Ouatiki, Abdelghani Boudhar, Marc Leblanc, Younes Fakir, Abdelghani
Chehbouni — *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*, 2022 —
[10.1016/j.ejrh.2022.101177](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2022.101177)
Finds that large wet-year peaks in GRACE terrestrial water storage can mask the
real rate of ongoing groundwater depletion in Morocco — a direct warning about
interpreting TWS anomalies naively.

**Sustainability of Morocco's groundwater resources in response to natural and
anthropogenic forces**
Mohamed Ahmed, Mourad Aqnouy, Jamal Stitou El Messari — *Journal of Hydrology*,
2021 — [10.1016/j.jhydrol.2021.126866](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2021.126866)
GRACE-based national assessment concluding no significant TWS or groundwater
trend over northern or southern Morocco as a whole. **Contradicts the depletion
findings above** — the disagreement is itself a research opening.

**Evaluation of GRACE and GRACE-FO derived-products for water storage assessment
in Moroccan aquifers: analysis of drought and human-induced impacts**
Mouad Abbad, Abdelghani Hadri, El Mahdi El Khalki, Hamza Ouatiki, Ismail Hanadé
Houmma, Ouma Jaffar, Taha El Ghazlani, Mohamed Qachar, Lhoussaine Bouchaou,
Ahmed El-Azhari, Kamal Yaalaoui, Abdelghani Chehbouni — *Geocarto International*,
2025 — [10.1080/10106049.2025.2521829](https://doi.org/10.1080/10106049.2025.2521829)
Benchmarks several GRACE/GRACE-FO products against piezometers in Moroccan
aquifers and finds the best product differs by basin — so product choice is a
decision we must justify, not a default.

**Estimating groundwater abstraction for irrigation in a data-scarce semi-arid
region using optical and InSAR time-series analysis: the Tensift catchment,
Morocco**
Youssef Hajhouji, Abdelhakim Amazirh, Wassim Mohamed Baba, Marieme Seif-Ennasr,
Mohamed Serraj, Salah Er-Raki — *Frontiers in Water*, 2026 —
[10.3389/frwa.2026.1917387](https://doi.org/10.3389/frwa.2026.1917387)
Sentinel-1 InSAR (2015–2025) plus optical data in the Ouled Gaid irrigated
perimeter; reports subsidence up to ~6.5 mm/yr driven by pumping and correlation
of 0.39–0.71 with estimated abstraction. The template for our planned InSAR
component.

**Moroccan Groundwater Resources and Evolution with Global Climate Changes**
Mohammed Hssaisoune, Lhoussaine Bouchaou, Abdelfettah Sifeddine, Ilham
Bouimetarhan, Abdelghani Chehbouni — *Geosciences*, 2020 —
[10.3390/geosciences10020081](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences10020081)
National review of Moroccan aquifers reporting 20–65 m water-table declines over
30 years across the Souss, Haouz, Tadla and Saïss plains. Useful as the
non-satellite ground truth for aquifer context.

## CHIRPS rainfall: validity in Morocco

**CHIRPS precipitation open data for drought monitoring: application to the
Tensift basin, Morocco**
Nabil Habitou, Ali Morabbi, Driss Ouazar, Ahmed Bouziane, Moulay Hasnaoui, Hicham
Sabri — *Journal of Applied Remote Sensing*, 2020 —
[10.1117/1.JRS.14.034526](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JRS.14.034526)
Validates CHIRPS against 11 Tensift stations (1990–2017), reporting monthly
correlations of 0.56–0.86. The single most directly useful reference for whether
CHIRPS is trustworthy in our study region.

**Assessment of Satellite and Reanalysis Precipitation Data Using Statistical and
Wavelet Analysis in Semi-Arid, Morocco**
Achraf Chakri, Nour-Eddine Laftouhi, Lahcen Zouhri, Hassan Ibouh, Mounir
Ibnoussina — *Water*, 2025 — [10.3390/w17111714](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17111714)
Compares CHIRPS against other satellite and reanalysis products over semi-arid
Morocco; relevant for choosing between CHIRPS and ERA5-Land.

**The Climate Hazards Center Infrared Precipitation with Stations, Version 3**
Chris Funk, Pete Peterson, Laura Harrison, Robert Saldivar, Martin Landsfeld,
Diego Pedreros, Shraddhanand Shukla, Andreas Fink, Frank Davenport, Seth
Peterson, William Turner, Austin Sonnier — *Scientific Data*, 2026 —
[10.1038/s41597-026-07096-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07096-4)
The CHIRPS v3 dataset paper, which includes a Tensift (Morocco) case study
reporting that v3 captures observed rainfall variability better than v2, and
notes station data should be gauge-undercatch-corrected when validating against
it. Decides the v2-versus-v3 question for us.

**morocco-water-stress** (Bouchra159) — GitHub —
[github.com/Bouchra159/morocco-water-stress](https://github.com/Bouchra159/morocco-water-stress)

# Gaps this project could fill

Ranked by how defensible each is given our MVP and stated roadmap.

### 1. Optical cross-validation of the radar reservoir record
Baba et al. (2025) is the authoritative Moroccan reservoir time series and it is
**Sentinel-1 radar**. Bounif et al. (2023) and Benzougagh et al. (2022) use
optical data but over different periods and with different indices. Nobody
published an independent Sentinel-2 optical series for Al Massira that is
explicitly compared against the radar record. Two independent sensors agreeing
(or disagreeing in a characterisable way) on the same reservoir is a real
contribution, and radar-versus-optical disagreement during the 2022–2024 minimum —
when the lake is shallow, turbid and fringed by wet mud that radar and NDWI treat
differently — is exactly where it would show up.

### 2. A reproducible, open pipeline rather than a published figure
Every Moroccan study above reports results; none ships a runnable pipeline that
someone else can point at a different dam. Our planned datacube tool — STAC query,
offset correction, masking, thresholding, area series, sanity checks — would be
the reusable artifact the literature lacks. This is also where the engineering
rules in CLAUDE.md earn their place.

### 3. The processing-baseline discontinuity as a documented pitfall
The January 2022 Sentinel-2 baseline-04.00 offset lands in the **middle** of the
drought, at the exact moment reservoir levels collapsed. None of the papers found
discusses it. A short, explicit demonstration of how much the uncorrected offset
biases an NDWI-derived area trend would be genuinely useful to anyone doing
multi-year Sentinel-2 time series, and it is cheap for us to produce as a
by-product.

### 4. Resolving the GRACE contradiction with an independent signal
Ouatiki et al. (2022) say depletion is being masked by climate variability;
Ahmed et al. (2021) find no significant national trend. Hajhouji et al. (2026)
show InSAR subsidence works as a pumping proxy, but only in one irrigated
perimeter in Tensift. Using InSAR subsidence as an *arbiter* between the two GRACE
readings, basin by basin, is an open question — and a well-defined one.

### 5. Joint basin-by-basin response, not one variable at a time
The literature is siloed: reservoirs in one paper, GRACE in another, NDVI in a
third, each over its own period and basin. Our framing question — how reservoirs,
groundwater and farmland responded **together**, per basin, over one common window
— is not answered anywhere found. Specifically unaddressed: whether basins
substitute groundwater for surface water when reservoirs fail (reservoir drawdown
followed by a GRACE/InSAR depletion signal and *sustained* NDVI), versus simply
contracting irrigated area (NDVI falls with reservoir storage). That is a
testable, per-basin signature, and it is the most interesting thing on this list.

### 6. The 2025–26 recovery
Published work stops in 2024. If the 2025–26 wet period is real, nobody has yet
characterised how much storage and farmland recovered, or whether groundwater
recovered at all — depletion is typically far slower to reverse. Our window runs
to the present, so this is ours by default.

## Caveats on this review

- Searches were in English. French and Arabic Moroccan literature, and ABHOER
  (Oum Er-Rbia basin agency) grey literature and official reservoir-level
  records, were not searched and may well cover gap 1 or gap 6.
- Several entries above are abstract-only. Before leaning on any of them in a
  write-up, read the full text.
- **ASSUMPTION:** that Baba et al. (2025) do not already publish an optical
  comparison in their supplementary material. Verify by reading the full paper
  before claiming gap 1.

## Prior art bearing on the gap list

**GloLakes: water storage dynamics for 27 000 lakes globally from 1984 to present
derived from satellite altimetry and optical imaging**
*Earth System Science Data*, 2024 —
[10.5194/essd-16-201-2024](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/201/2024/)
Global lake/reservoir **storage** (not just area) from altimetry plus Landsat
extent, 1984–present, including absolute storage via a geostatistical depth
model. A 2025 HESS paper fitted curves for 6,000+ structures from it.
**Check whether Al Massira is included before investing in a volume method.**

**Global Characterization of Inland Water Reservoirs Using ICESat-2 Altimetry and
Climate Reanalysis** — Ryan et al., *Geophysical Research Letters*, 2020 —
[10.1029/2020GL088543](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020GL088543)
ICESat-2 water levels against gauges at ~14 cm RMSE across 3,712 reservoirs.
Establishes feasibility of the altimetry route.

**Monitoring water level and volume changes of lakes and reservoirs in the Yellow
River Basin using ICESat-2 laser altimetry and Google Earth Engine**
*Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*, 2022 —
[S1570644322000466](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1570644322000466)
Pairs ICESat-2 levels with optical area for volume change at <9% uncertainty.
The nearest template for a satellite volume series.

**Estimating water levels in reservoirs using Sentinel-2 derived time series of
surface water areas: a case study of 20 reservoirs in Burkina Faso**
*Int. J. Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation*, 2025 —
[S1569843225001700](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1569843225001700)
Inverts Sentinel-2 area series to water level in a semi-arid African setting —
directly transferable, and avoids altimetry entirely.

**Monitoring Indian ungauged small reservoirs volume from remote sensing:
Feasibility, bias and perspectives** — *PLOS Water*, 2023 —
[10.1371/journal.pwat.0000260](https://journals.plos.org/water/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pwat.0000260)
Reports ~17% MAPE on area and ~31% on volume for small reservoirs, and that
standing water blocks ICESat-2 bathymetry. Honest error bars to compare against.

**Sentinel-1&2 Multitemporal Water Surface Detection** — IRD —
[PDF](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/2021-10/010082763.pdf)
Finds both sensors carry a negative reservoir-area bias that grows as the
reservoir nears full, with optical ahead of radar on recall. **This is the paper
that downgrades original gap 1** — the generic radar-versus-optical comparison is
already done. Author list unconfirmed.

**Quantitative mapping of groundwater depletion at the water management scale
using a combined GRACE/InSAR approach** — *Remote Sensing of Environment*, 2017 —
[S0034425717305746](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425717305746)
Uses InSAR as a spatial prior to sharpen GRACE groundwater trends. **Downgrades
original gap 4** — this is an established method, and it names the blocker:
converting InSAR displacement to storage loss needs lithological data that is
usually unavailable.

**Assessing Groundwater Depletion and Dynamics Using GRACE and InSAR: Potential
and Limitations** —
[ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307476419)
Companion limitation review; notes InSAR only applies where aquifers deform
measurably. Metadata unconfirmed.

**Monitoring groundwater and terrestrial water storage with GRACE/GRACE-FO across
MENA and the Gulf Cooperation Council countries: a regional review**
*Groundwater for Sustainable Development*, 2026 —
[S2214581826005471](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214581826005471)
Regional review proposing an uncertainty-aware GRACE framework. Read before any
GRACE work; also further evidence gap 4 is crowded.

**Mapping drought severity impact on arboriculture systems over Tadla and lower
Tassaout plains in Morocco using Sentinel-2 data and machine learning approaches**
*Geocarto International*, 2025 —
[10.1080/10106049.2025.2471104](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10106049.2025.2471104)
Sentinel-2 drought-impact mapping on perennial crops in Tadla — **inside the Oum
Er-Rbia basin**, downstream of our reservoir. The closest existing work to the
farmland half of the project.

**Study of agricultural land use variability in Doukkala irrigated area between
1998 and 2020** —
[ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357812400)
Reports NDVI decline tied to insufficient irrigation water and ~8% of cropland
converting to non-cropland. The best available lead on the *contraction* signature
in Al Massira's own command area. Attribution unconfirmed from the abstract;
metadata unconfirmed.

**Integrated ETₐ–NDVI Assessment for Sustainable Agricultural Water Management in
the Doukkala Irrigated Perimeter, Morocco** — reproducibility package —
[Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/22121528)
Covers Faregh, Sidi Bennour and Zemamra districts, 2015–2023, linking actual
evapotranspiration to vegetation dynamics. **Ships code and data** — worth reading
as a model for our own reproducibility, and it overlaps our farmland window.

**Remote Sensing and Meteorological Indexes of Drought Using Open Short
Time-Series Data in Doukkala Region, Morocco** — *Ecological Engineering &
Environmental Technology* —
[link](https://www.ecoeet.com/Remote-Sensing-and-Meteorological-Indexes-of-Drought-Using-Open-Short-Time-Series,156962,0,2.html)
Landsat NDVI with SPI/VCI/TCI/VHI over Doukkala; reports a weak NDVI–SPI
correlation. Useful negative result: vegetation indices and meteorological
drought indices do not track each other well here, which matters for how we
interpret NDVI.

**Operational monitoring of surface water dynamics in Algerian reservoirs with
remote sensing and ground validation** — *AQUA*, 2026 —
[10.2166/aqua.2026.081](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/operational-monitoring-of-surface-water-dynamics-in-algerian-reservoirs-with-remote-sensing-and-ground-validation/)
Operational reservoir monitoring with ground validation in neighbouring Algeria.
Nearest regional analogue to the pipeline we intend to build; metadata
unconfirmed, located via a citation index rather than the publisher.

# Third pass: found during blocker verification (2026-11-03)

**Seasonal forecasting of dam water resources using optimized hybrid models under
unprecedented drought conditions**
Ismaguil Hanadé Houmma, Abdessamad Hadri, Abdelghani Boudhar, El Mahdi El Khalki,
Ismail Karaoui, Sabir Oussaoui, Mohamed Samih, Christophe Kinnard —
*Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies*, 2026 —
[10.1016/j.ejrh.2025.103091](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrh.2025.103091) ·
[open access on HAL](https://hal.science/hal-05642023v1)
**The most important paper for this project after Baba et al.** Forecasts Al
Massira's monthly reservoir *volume* 1–6 months ahead with explainable-AI models
(ProbLSTM, GAM, LightGBM; Optuna tuning) driven by teleconnection indices, drought
indices and hydroclimatic variables. Best skill 86.2%, NMAE 3.6%, MAE 17.7 Mm³.
Uses **no satellite surface area**, so it is complementary rather than competing —
but it proves an active Moroccan group holds in-situ monthly volumes for this dam.

**Use of Spatial Remote Sensing to Study the Temporal Evolution of the Water
Retention of Al Massira Dam in Morocco** — Bounif, Rahimi, Boutafoust, El Mjiri —
*J. Ecological Engineering* 24(2), 340–349, 2023 —
[10.12911/22998993/156666](https://doi.org/10.12911/22998993/156666) ·
[open-access PDF](https://www.jeeng.net/pdf-156666-84220)
**Now read in full** (previously abstract-only). Landsat 4/5 TM, 7 ETM+, 8 OLI via
Google Earth Engine, Roy et al. (2016) band harmonisation, combined-index
thresholding (MNDWI ≥ 0, NDWI ≥ 0, NDVI ≤ 0, AWEI ≥ 0), **one August–September
